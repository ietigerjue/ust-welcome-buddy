This note summarizes practical postgraduate housing information for new HKUST students, especially RPG students. Housing rules, eligibility, fees, dates, and room availability can change, so always verify the latest details with HKUST Student Housing and Residential Life Office (SHRLO) and official HKUST housing pages.

## Who can apply

HKUST postgraduate housing is different from undergraduate halls. The MSSS source describes PG housing mainly for Research Postgraduate students, including MPhil and PhD students. Taught Postgraduate students should not assume they are eligible for these PG halls unless HKUST officially says so.

For official questions such as eligibility, application windows, check-in dates, room assignment, fees, and cancellation rules, use SHRLO as the primary source.

## Main PG housing options

Common PG housing options mentioned in the source are:

- SKCC, also known as Stephen Kam Chuen Cheong Hall or PG Hall 1.
- UA, short for University Apartment, especially Tower A and Tower B.
- GGT, short for Jockey Club Global Graduate Tower.

Each option feels different:

- SKCC is closer to some campus facilities and usually feels more like a traditional hall with single rooms and shared floor facilities.
- UA / Tower is suite-style. A suite usually has bedrooms plus a shared living room, kitchen, bathroom, and shower. It can feel more like shared apartment living.
- GGT is newer and generally has more modern facilities. It may be attractive for students who prefer a newer room and shared facilities, but walking routes and location should be considered.

## How to compare halls

When choosing preferences, think beyond "new or old":

- Location: check whether the hall is closer to your lab, department office, business school, sports facilities, LG7, LG1, or common bus routes.
- Room style: decide whether you prefer a private room with floor facilities or suite-style living with a few flatmates.
- Cooking and water: some buildings have kitchens or common rooms; facilities vary by hall.
- Laundry: laundry arrangements differ by building and may require online purchase or top-up through the current laundry system.
- Humidity: coastal rooms and lower floors can feel humid. New students should prepare dehumidifying habits and basic cleaning supplies.
- Noise and social life: suite-style halls may involve more roommate interaction; traditional halls may feel quieter or more independent.

## Application and allocation

Housing application and allocation are managed by SHRLO. The source stresses that PG housing allocation should not be treated as first-come-first-served unless the official current-year notice says so. Students should fill preferences carefully, but final allocation depends on SHRLO.

Room preference matching is not guaranteed. If you want to live with a specific person or prefer a certain hall, submit the request through official forms if available, but keep expectations flexible.

## Fees and extra costs

Housing fees, electricity rules, air-conditioning charging, and laundry charges may vary by hall and by year. Do not rely on old screenshots or student-shared numbers as official current fees.

Practical reminders:

- Check official SHRLO fee tables for the current academic year.
- Understand whether utilities are included or charged separately.
- If you receive PGS or stipend, confirm whether housing charges are deducted automatically or billed separately.
- Keep payment records and screenshots when paying housing-related fees.
- For laundry, check the current hall laundry guide before relying on older payment instructions.

## Move-in preparation

Before moving in:

- Read your official housing offer carefully.
- Check the earliest check-in date and whether late check-in needs notice.
- Bring or buy bedding that fits the hall bed size after confirming the room type.
- Prepare adapters, extension cords, basic cleaning supplies, laundry detergent, hangers, and dehumidifying products.
- After arrival, inspect the room and report serious facility problems through the official hall maintenance channel.

## Practical tip

Do not optimize only for hall popularity. For a new student, the best choice may be the hall that makes your daily routine easier: shorter walk to lab, reliable cooking or dining access, manageable humidity, and compatible roommate expectations.

For housing eligibility, fees, accommodation dates, hall rules, and check-in/check-out requirements, always verify with HKUST SHRLO and official HKUST housing sources.
